## 1. Research / Project Question

> **Do monsoon-region rainfall patterns show significant variance trends across Indian
> subdivisions, and can we detect 1-in-20-year deficits?**

VARSA (**V**ariability, **A**nomalies and **R**egional **S**tatistics **A**nalysis of Indian
rainfall) asks five concrete questions:

- **RQ1 — Seasonality.** How concentrated is rainfall in the June–September monsoon?
- **RQ2 — Regional variability.** How much do annual variability and reliability (CV) differ between subdivisions?
- **RQ3 — Distributions.** Which probability distributions describe rainfall totals well?
- **RQ4 — Trends.** Which subdivisions show a long-term trend after multiple-testing correction?
- **RQ5 — Drought risk.** How deep is a 1-in-20-year deficit, and where?

---

## 2. Why This Question

- India's rainfall arrives in a few intense months; a single national average hides how
  reliable it is in each region. Dry years are part of the record — how often they occur,
  and where, matters for farming and water planning.
- Answering it enables **agricultural planning and water-resource management**: regional
  analysis reveals patterns that national averages hide.
- The stakes of a wrong answer: treating a noisy series as trending (or a stable one as
  failing) misdirects drought policy. A significant-looking result is not automatically
  strong evidence — probability models let us state how confident each claim is.

## 3. Scope & Boundaries

| In scope | Out of scope |
|---|---|
| 36 IMD meteorological subdivisions, 1901–2015 | District- or grid-level analysis |

## 5. Methods (high-level)

- Seasonal means/shares and one-way ANOVA (RQ1)
- Per-division CV; Levene test → **Welch ANOVA** + Games–Howell-style comparisons (RQ2)
- Normal / Gamma / Lognormal fits judged by Kolmogorov–Smirnov distance (RQ3)
- OLS + Mann–Kendall per division; **Benjamini–Hochberg** correction over the 36 tests (RQ4)
- Deficit counts and empirical 5th percentile as the 1-in-20-year level (RQ5)
- Tools: Python (pandas / NumPy / SciPy / Plotly / Streamlit); all figures programmatic
